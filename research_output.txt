September 11 attacks (9/11) Summary:

Date: September 11, 2001
Location: United States (New York City, Arlington, Virginia; Pennsylvania crash site)
Perpetrators: Al‑Qaeda, 19 hijackers
Key Events:
- 08:46 a.m.: American Airlines Flight 11 crashes into the North Tower of the World Trade Center (WTC).
- 09:03 a.m.: United Airlines Flight 175 crashes into the South Tower of the WTC.
- 09:37 a.m.: American Airlines Flight 77 crashes into the Pentagon.
- 10:03 a.m.: United Airlines Flight 93 crashes in a field near Shanksville, PA after passengers revolt.
Outcomes:
- Both WTC towers collapse within 2 hours, killing 2,753 people at the site; total deaths 2,977 (including 246 on the four planes).
- 343 firefighters and 72 law‑enforcement officers killed – the deadliest day for U.S. first responders.
- Immediate grounding of all U.S. air traffic; worldwide security overhauls.
- U.S. launched the Global War on Terror, invading Afghanistan to dismantle al‑Qaeda.
- Long‑term health effects for survivors and responders; extensive economic and political impact.
- Al‑Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden claimed responsibility; killed in 2011.

Significance: The deadliest terrorist attack in history, prompting major changes in global security policy, international relations, and U.S. foreign‑policy strategy.
